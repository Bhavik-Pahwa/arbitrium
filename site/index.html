The part everyone rushes

Most arbitration clauses are never actually decided. They're copied.

A seat picked because the template had one. An institution named because someone remembered the acronym. Lawyers call the result a pathological clause: ambiguous, contradictory, or missing the one detail that matters. It doesn't fail at signing. It fails when you need it most, triggering jurisdictional objections, delays, and a fight over enforcement instead of the dispute itself.
